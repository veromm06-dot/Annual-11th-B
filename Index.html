I) Course Description

Area Purpose and General Aim

English is a course which seeks for an advanced development in the English language by applying the four skills and key principles like:

  • Inquiry-based learning
  • Student-centered learning
  • Explore global values
  • Focus on thinking, not memorizing
  • Build strong student-teacher relationships
  • Take time to reflect
  • Make connections with new knowledge
  • Cooperation and synergy

It is divided into ten lessons which include Reading to learn, language in use, life skills, and writing. Every unit also contains a project to develop according to different topics and contexts.

II) General Objectives

Understanding Reality and Critical Thinking
  • To improve Reading skills.
  • To apply Academic Word Lists (AWL) in different prepared activities.
  • To improve Listening skills.
  • To improve Speaking skills and speech delivery.
  • To improve Writing skills.
  • To synergize applying the different skills, taking advantage of Oxford Discover Futures 5.

V) Didactic Methods (Institutional Model)

Double Period Model (70 Minutes)
  • Engaging Introduction: Prior knowledge activation through icebreakers, energizers, and brainstorming.
  • Individual Work: Development of individual student potential, analytical skills, and language competencies.
  • Teamwork: Continuous interaction with assigned roles under the principle "all for one and one for all".
  • Plenary & Debrief: Public presentations, structured debates, Socratic dialogue, and flipped classroom discussions.
Key Methodologies Applied in Class
  • The Four Moments Methodology & Flipped Classroom: Preparatory reading at home to maximize practical, active learning in class.
  • Constructivism: The student acts as the primary protagonist in constructing their own knowledge.
  • Speech Presentations: Structured public speaking environments aimed at building fluency, clarity, and argumentative skills.
  • Project-Based Learning (PBL): Real-world, collaborative projects integrated into each instructional unit.

VII) Thematic Sequence by Units

UNIT I Topics & Strategies
  • Why are animals important? Reading strategy: Identifying tone. Grammar: Talking about things in general / Making comparisons. Listening: Listening to summarize an argument. Writing: Generating ideas and supporting opinions (Language point: Signpost language to paraphrase or clarify).
  • What’s below the surface? Reading strategy: Understanding subject-specific vocabulary. Grammar: Reporting suggestions; asking politely for information. Speaking: Telling engaging personal anecdotes. Writing: Writing a process essay.
  • How do we use patterns? Reading strategy: Recognizing idiomatic language. Grammar: Making and reporting predictions. Writing: Describing a line graph.
UNIT II Topics & Strategies
  • How do we solve problems? Reading strategy: Synthesizing information from multiple sources. Grammar: Describing ability and success; expressing criticism. Life Skill: Thinking outside the box. Speaking: Generating ideas as a group. Writing: Writing a problem-and-solution essay.
  • How does advertising work? Reading strategy: Identifying supporting details. Grammar: Avoiding repetition. Speaking: Asking for more details. Writing: Writing a promotional leaflet for an event.
  • Why do we need other people? Reading strategy: Comparing genres. Grammar: Talking about ongoing actions and states; talking about annoying habits and situations. Listening: Taking notes with mind maps.
  • Project: How can we take care of our mental health? Designing a social media campaign.
UNIT III Topics & Strategies
  • Why are we fascinated by space? Reading strategy: Visualizing a description. Grammar: Talking about purpose. Life Skill: Problem-solving as a team. Listening: Listening for use of the dramatic present. Writing: Making a biography more memorable.
  • What do we know? Reading strategy: Identifying assumptions. Grammar: Hedging. Listening: Using the introduction to help you make notes. Writing: A persuasive blog post.
  • Project: Is it more important to explore space or the oceans?
UNIT IV Topics & Strategies
  • How should we respond to crime? Reading strategy: Evaluating an author’s arguments. Grammar: Talking about hypothetical situations and consequences. Speaking: Advising about staying safe. Writing: Writing a persuasive cause-and-effect essay.
  • What matters most to you? Reading strategy: Recognizing rhetorical devices. Grammar: Expressing choice with -ever; adding information using relative clauses with which. Life Skill: Developing a growth mindset. Writing: Bringing a story to life.
